Global Motion Transport — 90-Day Authorization Hold

Findings from a full audit of the deposit-hold system, the Visa rulebook, and Stripe's authorization products — plus the plan we propose.
The plan, in two lines
  1. Keep the hold alive by re-placing it before each expiry. A scheduled job cancels the expiring authorization and places a fresh one on the same card, so coverage rolls forward for the full 90+ days. The network ceiling is 30 days at best, and GMT's account currently looks closer to a week — so a 90-day rental means somewhere between 4 and 18 of these cycles, each one a fresh chance for the card to decline. But we fix one bug first — today, cancelling a hold cancels the entire rental, and the chain would trigger that every few days.
  2. Change what the hold is for. It covers unpaid rent — which the card rules permit — and not damage, which they forbid at any duration. The rental agreement is updated to tell the renter the hold will be re-placed. Damage is meant to move to a paid damage waiver, but that is not in the schedule below: selling one involves state licensing rules and price caps we have not checked, and Bonzah's agreement to it. Until it exists, GMT recovers damage only by an after-the-fact claim — see decision 3.

What we found

Two things, and the second was a surprise.

1
A single authorization can never exceed 30 days — but chaining them is legitimate

Confirmed from Stripe's documentation and Visa's own published rules directly: the ceiling is 30 days on every network (Visa is exactly 29 days 18 hours; Mastercard, Amex and Discover 30). That ceiling is set by the card networks, not by Stripe, so no processor can compliantly offer a 90-day hold. However, nothing prohibits chaining, and the rules assume it. Visa caps a vehicle rental authorization at 30 days and separately requires a merchant to release it once that period ends — so a rental longer than 30 days can only be covered by a sequence of authorizations, and Visa's merchant guidance for extended rentals describes exactly that: close the original within its validity period and request a new one. So GMT can have 90 days of continuous coverage; they cannot have one unbroken 90-day hold. We already have this mechanism built — it just isn't reliable enough to run it 4 to 18 times per rental.

2
The real constraint is not duration — it is what the hold is allowed to cover

We downloaded the Visa Core Rules and Product & Service Rules, 18 April 2026 edition and read it directly. Rule 5.7.2.4 states an estimated authorization "does not include any amount covering potential damage, theft, other delayed charges, insurance premiums, or tips." Rule 5.8.8.4 — the Vehicle Rental Merchant Asset Protection Program, which exists specifically to let rental companies over-authorize "to protect them from potential losses associated with extended rental periods" — repeats that the request "must not include an amount to cover for potential damage, theft, or other delayed charge."

So a compliant card hold for damage does not exist at 90 days, at 30 days, or at 7 days. This is a live exposure today, independent of duration. Corroborated independently: SIXT's own help centre states their deposit "does not cover damage claims" and routes damage to a protection package instead.

Visa's sanctioned route for damage is a delayed charge within 90 calendar days of the rental return, with documentation, a repair quote, the renter's right to an alternative estimate, and a 20-business-day wait. The 90 days GMT wants does exist in the rules — on the other side of the rental.

Defects that must be fixed before the chain runs

Going from 1 cancellation per rental to an estimated 13–18 turns two latent bugs into routine events

The chained design is safe. The code it would run on is not — yet. These two are ship-blocking and are roughly a day of work.

A
Cancelling a hold cancels the entire rental

Our payment webhook reacts to a cancelled authorization by marking the payment refunded and then setting the rental's status to Cancelled — with no check on whether the rental is live. On any hold that has a payment record attached — which is exactly what defect B below creates — a routine nightly re-authorization on a 90-day rental would flip it to Cancelled mid-term: the vehicle could be re-listed, billing stops, revenue is reversed, and nobody would know why. Present in all three webhook files.

B
Holds placed via the portal button are booked as captured revenue

A hold created through the "Add Hold" checkout is misclassified by the webhook as a completed payment. It marks the rental as paid, writes a payment record for the full amount that was never actually captured, and allocates it against real rent. Collected revenue is overstated, balances zero out, and payment reminders stop. Under the new design — where the hold legitimately is anticipated rent — this looks plausible, which makes it far harder to spot.

The full audit registered 86 edge cases across the Stripe lifecycle, card and customer changes, rental lifecycle, amounts, infrastructure, compliance, reconciliation and customer experience. They are documented with handling in docs/GMT_CHAINED_HOLD_SPEC.md.

Decisions we need

1. URGENT — the UAE migration and GMT now collide.

A Stripe authorization, customer and saved card cannot move between platform accounts. With 60–120 day rentals starting before the previous one ends, GMT's migration readiness check will essentially never go green — and it is currently bypassable by a "Flip anyway" button in the admin panel. The UAE migration owner needs to know GMT is now a 120-day problem, before a flip strands live holds. This is the most time-sensitive item in this document.

2. What may the hold cover, precisely? (needs counsel)

Unpaid and anticipated rent is unambiguously permitted — it is what the renter will genuinely spend. Fuel, mileage overage and late-return fees are arguably delayed charges, which the same rules exclude. We are building to rent only until this is answered, because putting the others into a documented formula would make a violation provable from our own code.

3. Will GMT run a damage-claims process?

Taking damage out of the hold removes the only recovery mechanism that exists today. If GMT will not run claims, damage recovery is limited to the waiver plus a fleet insurance policy — a pricing decision about who absorbs small claims, not an engineering gap.

4. Which state does GMT operate in, and is a 90-day rental one agreement or chained 30-day extensions?

Their state is currently inferred from an old marketing script, not their tenant record. New York bans card deposits for damage outright on agreements of 30 days or less — so chaining short extensions could pull every segment inside a rule that a single long agreement sits outside. Only GMT can tell us how they paper these.

5. Does GMT accept debit cards on rentals over 30 days?

Each cycle releases one authorization and requests another, and how fast a release clears is the renter's bank's decision, not ours — so two pending items for the same rental will routinely be visible at once. On a credit card that is confusing; on a debit card it is money the renter genuinely cannot spend, up to 18 times over a 90-day rental. Clearer wording on the statement and a notification per cycle reduce it; nothing we can build removes it. Our audit rates this the most likely source of "I don't recognise this charge" disputes.
